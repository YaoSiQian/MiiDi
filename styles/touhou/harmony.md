# Harmony Vocabulary

Bright minor-key vocabulary with rock cadences, rooted in A minor as the
default tonal center. Chord analysis of 180 MIDI files confirms minor-mode
dominance with 7th-chord extensions appearing frequently across the
multi-instrument harmonic bed.

## Core Chord Symbols

| symbol | quality | function |
|---|---|---|
| Am | minor | tonic (i) — most common home chord |
| Dm | minor | subdominant (iv) |
| Em | minor | minor dominant (v) |
| F | major | submediant (VI) |
| G | major | dominant (VII) — natural minor |
| E7 | dominant 7 | dominant (V7) — harmonic minor cadence |
| C | major | relative major (III) |
| Bdim | diminished | leading-tone (vii°) |
| Dm7 | min7 | subdominant with color |
| Asus4 | suspended | tension before tonic resolution |

## Characteristic Progressions

- **ZUN chorus loop**: Am - F - G - Am
  The signature four-chord cycle. Appears in verses and choruses across nearly
  every Touhou arrangement. The VI-VII-i motion drives relentless forward energy.

- **Royal road descent**: Am - G - F - E7
  Stepwise bass descent resolving to the harmonic minor dominant. Creates the
  bittersweet tension-and-release ZUN favors for emotional peaks.

- **Relative lift**: Dm - E7 - Am
  iv-V7-i in harmonic minor. Strongest cadential motion — use for the moment
  the melody lands hardest.

- **Circle-of-fifths fragment**: Am - Dm - G - C
  Common in transitional passages and bridge sections. Brightens momentarily
  before returning to minor territory.

- **Pedal dominant**: E7 - E7 - Am
  Sustained dominant tension resolved by abrupt tonic arrival. Works for
  section transitions and dramatic openings.

## Key Centers

The corpus gravitates toward A minor and D minor as primary tonal centers,
with E minor appearing in slower pieces. Relative major keys (C, F, Bb)
surface in brighter or more playful arrangements. Accidentals from the
harmonic minor scale (G# in A minor) appear at cadences.

## Cadences

Choruses end on a hard authentic close: E7 - Am with full-band accent on the
arrival. Verses can half-cadence on G or suspend on Asus4 to keep momentum.
Avoid soft resolutions — every cadence lands loud and on the grid.

The E7-to-Am cadence is the single most defining harmonic gesture in the
Touhou style. The raised third (G#) in E7 creates a leading tone that
pulls semantically toward A minor, producing the bright-yet-melancholic
quality characteristic of ZUN's music.
